UK Retail Sales & Inflation Analysis (2017–2023)

## Project Overview
This project analyses the impact of the UK cost-of-living crisis on retail sales across different sectors. It uses R to clean ONS retail sales data and CPIH/HCI inflation data, conducts descriptive statistics, correlation analysis, and sector-by-sector linear regression models, and visualises the results using ggplot2.

## Data Sources
**Retail Sales:** ONS Retail Sales Index (Chained volume, seasonally adjusted).
**Inflation:** ONS CPIH indices and Household Cost Index (HCI).
(Note: The raw data files were removed to keep the repository clean. The cleaned datasets are provided as _clean.csv files).

## Key Files
Dissertation.R: The main R script for data cleaning, merging, analysis, and visualization.
regression_by_sector.csv: Results showing how inflation impacts different sectors.
retail_trend_annotated.png: Visualisation of retail trends highlighting the pandemic and inflation surge.
regression_hci_effect.png: Chart showing the effect of HCI on sales by sector.

## How to Run
Ensure you have the required R packages installed (`readxl`, `tidyr`, `dplyr`, `lubridate`, `janitor`, `ggplot2`, `broom`, `lmtest`, `car`).
Open Dissertation.R in RStudio.
Run the script to reproduce the analysis and charts.


## Key Visualisations & Findings

Here are the key charts generated by my analysis:

### 1. Retail Sales Trends by Sector
[Retail Trend Faceted](retail_trend_faceted.png)
Figure 1: UK Retail Sales Volume by Sector (2017–2023). The faceted chart shows that while food and online sales remained relatively stable or grew, sectors like clothing and household goods saw sharp drops during the pandemic and the subsequent cost-of-living crisis.

### 2. The Inflation Surge
[Inflation Annotated Chart](inflation_annotated_chart.png)
*Figure 2: CPI vs HCI (Household Cost Index). The chart highlights the exact timeline of the crisis. We can see a dramatic surge starting in late 2021, peaking in early 2023, with the Household Cost Index (HCI) rising much faster than the standard Consumer Prices Index (CPI).*

### 3. Impact of Inflation on Retail Sectors
[Regression HCI Effect](regression_hci_effect.png)
*Figure 3: Regression Analysis (HCI Coefficient). This chart shows how sensitive each sector is to inflation. A negative value means that as the Household Cost Index rises, retail sales fall. "Clothing" and "Household goods" are the most negatively affected, while "Food" is the least affected (because people must still eat).
